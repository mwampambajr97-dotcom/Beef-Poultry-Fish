Lab Safety and Health Rules

Please read carefully through the Lab Safety and Health Rules below. In case of any emergency, contact your lab supervisor immediately.

A. General B. Chemicals C. Warning Labels D. Accidents & Injuries E. Fire F. Emergency G. Biological Safety H. Broken Glass I. Cleaning

AGeneral

  1. Conduct all assignments and requests from the lecturer and/or technician promptly according to the above mentioned rules. Choosing shortcuts can be perilous.
  2. Open shoes, sandals, shorts, short skirts, ties and excessive clothing and long hair that is not tied u, can be dangerous and cause accidents and are thus not allowed. Long hair must please be tied up.
  3. Walking around the building barefoot is prohibited.
  4. Wearing protective clothing (clean white coats), safety glasses and equipment are compulsory when prescribed.
  5. Smoking, eating or drinking are not allowed in laboratories. Never store food in a refrigerator in which chemicals are stored.
  6. Do no sit or lie on laboratory work surfaces.
  7. Work intelligently and independently of other students as far as possible.
  8. Clean and neat laboratory practices are required as far as possible. Leave your work area and the rest of the laboratory clean and tidy.
  9. Bunsen burners must always be placed on asbestos slabs when used, to protect the work surfaces.
  10. Playing or horsing about in the laboratory is strictly prohibited.
  11. If something is unclear, please ask for more information.
  12. Handle apparatus in a professional manner and if you are not a hundred percent sure as to how the apparatus works, rather ask. Use balances carefully, clean after use and close the windows.
  13. Report all broken apparatus immediately to the supervisor or technician.
  14. Report any maintenance problems to the laboratory in charge or supervisor.
  15. Remember to fill out logbooks of apparatus.
  16. Do not ever slam or kick doors of incubators, ovens, freezers or fridges.

B Handling Chemicals

  1. All chemical solutions MUST be marked with the following information: type of chemical; concentration; date prepared; and student’s name (name and surname).
  2. NEVER put chemicals in unmarked containers.
  3. Never sniff chemicals directly.
  4. Sucking up liquids with a pipette using the mouth can be detrimental. Always use your pipette filler.
  5. Contact or accidental inhalation of dangerous substances must be reported to the supervisor immediately.
  6. Immediately rinse splashes from the skin using plenty of cold water; use polyethylene glycol to rinse off lipophilic substances. Never use organic substances. Since may be absorbed
  7. Corrosive substances entering the eye should be thoroughly rinsed out with a gentle of water (or using special eye-wash equipment). Hold the eyelids wide open and move the eyes in all directions. Consult an eye specialist immediately, stating the chemical substances involved.
  8. Immediately remove any clothing that has become contaminated with a corrosive substance.
  9. Be sure to seek medical advice in the event of an accident or if you feel unwell.
  10. Proper experimental planning is necessary, especially when working with flammable, poisonous or explosive substances.
  11. Do not experiment with chemicals. Tasting of chemicals is also prohibited.
  12. Fume cupboards must be used-especially if vapour of gasses that may be present, are unknown.
  13. When bottles containing dangerous substances are opened, wear eye protection and gloves.
  14. Do not carry chemical bottles by the lids. Use a special basket to carry chemical bottles.
  15. Do not pour reagents back into bottles it will cause contamination.
  16. Wipe any substances that have spilled immediately. Do not leave teardrops on bottles and do not switch pipettes between bottles.
  17. Wash all glassware thoroughly using soap and water. If glassware does not want to come clean, ask the supervisor for help.
  18. All apparatus must be left clean and your hands must be washed thoroughly if dangerous chemicals have been handled.

C Warning Labels

Before using chemicals, first read the label and check for WARNINGS. Familiarize yourself with the warnings and take the necessary precautions see the list of warning codes attached and look at the poster (in the upstairs hallway) explaining the safety symbols. When ordering new chemicals, get the Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) from the supplier.

D Accidents and Injuries

When you (or someone else) are involved in an accident or are injured, remember the following:

  1. Remain calm.
  2. If an acid or alkaline substance is spilled on the injured person, wash that area under the tap or emergency shower.
  3. If it is a cut or any other wound, call the supervisor and /or first aid person to help.
  4. Know where the nearest basin, emergency shower, eyewash bottle and the first aid box are situated.

EFire

  1. Follow the rules as for gas flames.
  2. Make sure that the gas burner taps are closed properly after use.
  3. Know where the closest fire extinguisher is and how to use it
  4. If possible, close the main gas valve (marked in yellow) in your laboratory, close all windows and close the door when everyone in the laboratory is out.

F Emergency Conditions

  1. Call the supervisor and emergency helpers.
  2. Know where the closest telephone is and phone your campus security or emergency contact to report the incident. (Contact details for this campus are still to be confirmed.)
  3. Give basic details so that they can properly inform the emergency services.
  4. Familiarize yourself with the emergency plan of the building, the evacuation procedures and the meeting-place.

G Biological Safety

  1. All biological materials have to be autoclaved – red bins are available for petri dishes. If the bins are full, clean autoclave bags should be taken from the in charge or laboratory supervisor. The bags are kept in the fridge until ¾ full, before being autoclaved.
  2. All tips (yellow and blue) must be autoclaved and may not be placed in regular waste bins under any circumstances. Anyone who breaks this policy will be fined. Special containers will be available in all laboratories and in the walk-in fridge for tips and sharps (eg needles).
  3. All glassware used for sludge, or used to grow bacteria, must be autoclaved before washing.
  4. When working with sludge, pathogenic bacteria or hazardous chemicals is compulsory to wear white coats, gloves, masks and goggles.
  5. You must work in fume cupboards when working with sludge, effluent or hazardous chemicals.

H Broken Glass

  1. Never put broken glass into the regular bin. Use the green or blue bins provided in each laboratory.
  2. Always clean glassware directly after use. Residue can harden and ay become difficult to remove especially in bottles.

I Cleaning the Laboratory

There are cleaners responsible for cleaning laboratory floors and garbage bins and the students are responsible for cleaning work surfaces and the rest of the laboratory. Students are responsible for cleaning everything they worked with.
