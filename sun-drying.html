Sun Drying Meat for Preservation

Drying removes moisture from meat so microorganisms cannot grow, allowing the product to be stored at room temperature without rapid spoilage. Moisture is tracked over a six-day drying period, checked every two days.

This is a preparation tool, not a substitute for the real lab. The real practical unfolds over six actual days — this page compresses that into one sitting so you can learn the checkpoint sequence and what a drying curve looks like. It cannot replace watching and weighing a real sample over real time.

Materials & Equipment

Materials

  • Meat steaks
  • Salt

Equipment

  • A sharp knife
  • Tray
  • Culinary scale
  • Marker
  1. Trim excess fat from the beef so the meat is lean, then weigh it.
  2. Cut the beef into a uniform shape, so a whole batch dries equally and simultaneously.
  3. Mark the weight of each cut individually.
  4. Season with salt to inhibit microbial growth (approx. 14% common salt solution).
  5. Analyze and record moisture using a moisture analyzer.
  6. Lay the pieces in the open sun to dry.
  7. Analyze moisture every two days, for six days.

No pass/fail is shown here, on purpose. The manual does not state a target final moisture percentage. In published guidance for dried meat (for example USDA jerky guidance and New Zealand biltong rules), the safety benchmark is water activity below about 0.85, not a moisture percentage — because the moisture figure that reaches that point changes with how much salt and sugar the meat contains. Your moisture-analyzer readings show that drying is happening; they do not on their own show the batch is safe to store.
