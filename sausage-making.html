Making of Uncooked, Smoked Sausages

Beef sausages are made from seasoned ground meat, stuffed in casings, and smoked. Uncooked, smoked sausages must be stored under refrigerated conditions afterward.

This is a preparation tool, not a substitute for the real lab. Working through this process here helps you learn the sequence and reasoning behind each step before your session — it cannot teach knife handling, grinder operation, or the hands-on judgement needed to actually produce a sausage batch. You must still complete this practical in person with your supervisor.

Materials

  • A sharp blade
  • Fillet meat (fresh, clean, not contaminated)
  • Spices and seasonings (nitrites, sugar, fresh and crushed)
  • Casings
  • Plastic gloves
  • Tape (to label beaker)

Equipment

  • Thermometer
  • Scale
  • Meat grinder
  • Bowl chopper / mixer
  • Sausage stuffer

Sample Preparation

  1. Cut the meat fillet into smaller sizes.
  2. Weigh the ingredients carefully before use.

Procedure

  1. For safety, keep the temperature of the meat as cold as possible.
  2. Wash the pieces of meat and place them in the grinder on sample arrival at the laboratory.
  3. Grind the various meats.
  4. Add the rest of the ingredients as a slurry, mixing thoroughly. The slurry is made of spices and salt with two cups of water — this dissolves the curing ingredients, aids mixing, and gives the product its characteristic texture and taste.
  5. Grind again to the desired consistency, distributing ingredients evenly and making particle size uniform.
  6. Place the ground mixture into casings (natural or manufactured) to shape it and hold it together for thermal processing.
  7. Smoke in three stages, then cool:
    60°C 1 hour
    →
    71°C 1 more hour
    →
    82°C 2 hours

    Target: internal temperature reaches 68°C. Remove from the smokehouse and let the sausages cool.
